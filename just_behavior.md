### Game-Theoretic View of Character, Observation, and Justice

**Premise**  
In social systems, observation is stochastic: you are sometimes watched, sometimes not.  
Maintaining separate behaviors for observed vs unobserved states increases complexity, risk, and variance.

**Key Insight**  
Adopting a single, consistent behavioral policy that performs well under observation *and* non-observation is a dominant strategy in repeated games.

**Interpretation**  
What is often called “character” is not moral ornamentation, but a **state-invariant strategy**:
- No need to detect whether you are being watched  
- No branching behavior trees  
- Lower cognitive and reputational entropy  

**Game-Theoretic Framing**
- Fairness acts as an error signal in social interactions  
- Justice is the corrective feedback loop triggered by observed imbalance  
- Observation raises the cost of defection by making actions legible and attributable  

**Strategic Advantages**
- Reduced long-term variance and regret  
- Greater trust from others due to predictability  
- Lower monitoring and enforcement overhead  
- Increased autonomy over time as oversight relaxes  

**Summary**
Adopt a behavioral strategy whose expected value is maximized across all probabilities of observation.  
Live as though you are sometimes watched, so it never matters whether you are.
