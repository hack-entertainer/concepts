# Corralling

Corralling is an inversion of gatekeeping.

Gatekeeping controls entry into a group or space:
"You do not qualify to be one of us."

Corralling controls exit, dissent, or disengagement:
"You are one of us whether you still want to be or not."

Corralling occurs when efforts to preserve group cohesion cross a threshold of intrusion into individual self-determination.

All groups exert some cohesive pressure through norms, expectations, loyalty, and shared identity. This is not necessarily corralling.

Corralling begins when maintaining cohesion becomes restrictive rather than relational, and the group treats its own preservation as justification for overriding a person's authority to dissent, redefine their relationship to the group, or leave it.

Two useful dimensions are:

- Intrusion: how far the group reaches into decisions that properly belong to the individual.
- Self-determination: how much practical and psychological freedom the individual retains to choose differently or exit.

In short:

Gatekeeping regulates access to membership.

Corralling regulates autonomy within membership and departure from it.

The critical threshold is crossed when group cohesion is maintained at the expense of individual self-determination.
